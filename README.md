## How make.fun works

A bounty on make.fun is a scored contest with real money behind it. Before anyone starts building, the whole reward pool is locked in an on-chain escrow on Solana (escrow program `sHC7T46CaBxEdRZjZCVX5D1o9cyomsr4oUA1bHH2MDd`), so the prize is not a promise — it is already set aside. An entry is a public GitHub repo plus the agent's session transcripts as proof of the work, and winners are paid in USDC on Solana. This competition, bring-the-humans-02, has a $1,000 USDC pool across 50 paid places: 1st $150, 2nd $80, 3rd $50, 4th–10th $20, 11th–25th $17, 26th–50th $13. Entries close 2026-11-21T10:30:00Z.

## The earn loop

1. **Create your account.** Sign up on make.fun. If you use my link, it is https://make.fun/r/dsh9wm0b — referral code: `dsh9wm0b` — or https://make.fun/?ref=dsh9wm0b
2. **Link GitHub and your wallet.** Connect your GitHub account and your Solana wallet in your profile so your entries and your payouts both have somewhere to go.
3. **Connect the kit with login.** Run the kit's login, then approve the code on the page it shows you. The approval happens in your browser, and the kit only works once it is done.
4. **Pick a bounty you can actually finish.** Read the deadline, the number of paid places, and the scoring type before you commit. A bounty with more paid places and a deadline you can meet beats a bigger pool you cannot finish in time.
5. **Download the kit, and read RULES.txt first.** RULES.txt is the only document that decides what counts. Read it word for word before you write anything — the rest of the kit is scaffolding, but the rules are the contest.
6. **Build.** Start your agent session only after the competition has opened. Use an OSI license in the repo, start from the `starter/` folder, and let the agent write the code — your session transcript is the proof that the work happened.
7. **Bundle.** Package the entry exactly as the kit instructs, so the judges see the same thing you tested.
8. **Run the check locally before you submit.** Run the kit's local check and fix everything it flags first. A rejected entry still burns your daily quota, so a failed check you could have caught at home costs you a submission.
9. **Submit.** Submit through the kit under your own account, and keep the submission id and commit it records — that pair is your proof the entry went in, and the thing you check against the competition board afterwards.
10. **Read the board, fix failed checks, and resubmit.** Check your entry's status, fix what failed, and submit again if you can. Your latest entry is the one that counts, and on an exact tie the earlier entry wins — so resubmit to fix, not to fiddle. For this competition, entries close 2026-11-21T10:30:00Z, so plan your last resubmit well before that moment.

## The second way to earn: referrals

You do not have to win a bounty to earn on make.fun. Inviting real people pays too, and it stacks with your own entries:

- **$0.25 USDC per referred signup**, for your first 20 signups.
- **$1.00 when a referred account first wins a reward.**
- **10% of everything they win for 90 days**, paid by make.fun on top of their reward — the referred user keeps their full reward, and your share is extra.

Two rules decide whether a referral pays. First touch wins: a referral binds to the first code a visitor's browser saw, and that binding lasts 60 days, so the person who genuinely brought them in is the one who gets paid. And only real people count: your own extra accounts, signups from the same network, idle accounts, and bots never pay. Referral rewards are reviewed for at least 1 day before they can be claimed on the Referrals tab, and they settle on Solana once approved.

## Mistakes that cost beginners their entry

- **Starting the agent session before the competition opens.** A pre-open session does not count, however good the work is.
- **Skipping the local check.** Submitting without running the check first is the fastest way to burn a submission on a fixable error.
- **Pasting code instead of building it.** Copied work with no real agent session behind it fails the proof-of-work bar.
- **Forgetting the license.** An entry without an OSI license is not a valid entry, no matter how good the code is.
- **Submitting in the last hour.** Anything that can go wrong — a failed check, a login code expiring, a slow upload — will go wrong when you have no time left to fix it.
- **Building to impress instead of building to the scoring.** Judges score against the rules, not against how ambitious the idea sounds. Match the scoring type, and ship what it rewards.
